## Project Overview

Reconstructed and refined an earlier University of North Texas analytics project examining international reserve data from the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

The original coursework used Google Cloud, BigQuery, SQL, and Tableau to prepare IMF reserve data and produce two primary analytical outputs:

- Top Reserve Holders (2023)
- Global Reserves Over Time (2020–2023)

For the portfolio version, the original workflow was rebuilt, validated, documented, and re-visualized while preserving the original analytical intent.

## Project Objectives

The reconstruction focused on:

- recovering the original data-preparation workflow;
- rebuilding the analytical dataset in Google BigQuery;
- reproducing the original SQL analysis;
- validating reporting entities and measurement definitions;
- recreating the original Tableau visualizations;
- improving presentation quality without changing the core coursework scope; and
- preserving the analytical workflow in a reproducible Git repository.

## Data Preparation

The IMF dataset contained reserve observations across countries, regional aggregates, institutions, indicators, and multiple time periods.

The reconstruction recreated the cleaned analytical dataset in BigQuery and used SQL `UNPIVOT` operations where required to convert year-based columns into an analysis-ready year/value structure.

Primary analytical attention was placed on annual observations from 2020 through 2023.

## SQL Reconstruction

The reconstructed SQL workflow was preserved as a sequence of version-controlled artifacts.

The core reconstruction included:

- rebuilding the year/value structure used in the original project;
- validating the 2023 gold-reserve analysis;
- removing regional and institutional aggregate entities from the country-level ranking;
- producing the cleaned Top 10 reserve-holder result; and
- rebuilding the 2020–2023 global-reserve trend.

Additional exploratory SQL was retained separately to examine physical gold volume, market valuation, and differences between reserve measures, but that work was kept outside the initial coursework reconstruction scope.

## Top Reserve Holders (2023)

The original coursework ranked reporting entities using the IMF indicator:

**Gold reserves at national valuation**

During reconstruction, the original result was found to include a regional aggregate among country-level entities.

For the portfolio version, regional and institutional aggregates were removed to produce a consistent country/jurisdiction comparison.

The resulting Top 10 were:

1. Germany
2. Italy
3. France
4. Russian Federation
5. China
6. Switzerland
7. Japan
8. Türkiye
9. India
10. Netherlands

Values are reported in **millions of U.S. dollars at national valuation**.

## Market Trends: Global Reserves (2020–2023)

The second original visualization examined changes in aggregate gold reserves at national valuation from 2020 through 2023.

The reconstructed totals were approximately:

The reconstructed visualization shows:

## Data Validation & Analytical Limitations

Reconstructing the project revealed an important limitation in the original analysis.

The IMF indicator **Gold reserves at national valuation** is expressed in U.S. dollars, but the underlying valuation basis follows national accounting practices.

This means the values are valid representations of the source data, but they should not be interpreted as a standardized comparison of either physical gold holdings or current market value across countries.

The reconstruction therefore distinguished between:

**data accuracy**

and

**comparability for a specific analytical question**

This limitation was documented as part of the reconstruction rather than used to redefine the scope of the original coursework.

## Reconstruction Improvements

The portfolio version improves the original project without replacing it with a different analysis.

Improvements include:

- removal of regional and institutional aggregates from the country ranking;
- explicit identification of the national-valuation measurement basis;
- rebuilt and documented BigQuery SQL;
- version-controlled analytical artifacts;
- cleaned and documented processed datasets;
- redesigned Tableau visualizations;
- improved titles, labels, units, and visual hierarchy; and
- preservation of the original analytical workflow as technical evidence.
